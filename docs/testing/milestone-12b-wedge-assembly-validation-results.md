# Milestone 12B: Independent Synthetic Assembly Validation for Wedge 41769a

## Executive summary

**GO for the smallest M12C offline experiment.** The bounded M12B harness
accepted every independently specified valid `41769a` assembly and rejected
every invalid fixture. It found no false-negative body collision in the tested
domain. This is evidence only for the exact vertical stud interfaces below; it
does not certify physical clutch, stability, insertion clearance, or production
export readiness.

## Variant and metadata provenance

The only experimental wedge is catalogue part `41769`, whose LDraw source is
the unchamfered `41769a` (*Wing 2 x 4 Right without Chamfer*), not `41769b`.
The checked-in source is from the LDraw May 2026 release, which lists those
variants separately.
The checked-in asset pins `parts/41769a.dat` to SHA-256
`0b069ab042f2cc8297a52e76b49a06e504cd4e9a51f02d6aea8c63f686c28ff8`.
M12A derived five female bottom-plane receiver centres from the inherited
`s/41770as01.dat` LDCad Shadow Library metadata (commit
`9b1131fb1991f8c0bfc072325e4e12f6271aba35`) after the source reflection and
the established LDraw-to-part-local transform:

`(0.5, 1.5)`, `(1.5, 1.5)`, `(2.5, 1.5)`, `(3.5, 1.5)`, `(3.5, 0.5)`.

Coordinates use studs in X/Y and plates in Z; a stud is 8 mm and a plate is
3.2 mm. Only 0, 90, 180, and 270 degree clockwise world rotations are
accepted; M12A rotates within the 4 x 2 local frame and then adds the integer
world placement `(x, y, z)` without rounding. The source-backed top studs are `(0.5, 1.5, 1)` through
`(3.5, 1.5, 1)`. The harness transforms these exact coordinates; it does not
infer a family rule or turn visual recesses into receivers.

## Harness

`backend/app/conversion/m12b_wedge_assembly_validation.py` is offline-only and
is not imported by converter V4--V7, APIs, workers, frontend code, or export
routing. It reuses M12A placement, analytic convex-polygon collision, and
connector matching rules. For each fixture it reports placements, collision
pairs, connection edges, unsupported indices, connected components, isolated
parts, invalid touching interfaces, part counts, and projection consistency.

Ground support, direct vertical-stud support, graph connectivity, and body
collision are intentionally distinct. A successful report never claims
structural stability. Current production export has no experimental wedge
route, so `exportRepresentable` is explicitly `None` rather than guessed.

## Collision, attachment, support, and connectivity

Collision uses M12A's continuous transformed wedge exterior polygon plus open
Z-interval overlap, not the wedge's 4 x 2 bounding box. A `3024` at `(4,1,0)`
shares only the bounding-box boundary with a wedge at `(0,0,0)` and is correctly
non-colliding. At `(3,0,0)` it has a positive-area intersection and is
correctly colliding. No false-negative collision occurred in these synthetic
cases.

Exact connector coincidence is mandatory. Therefore physical contact without a
source-backed male/receiver pair is reported as an invalid interface, never as
support. The complete three-part assembly has exactly one connected component,
zero unsupported placements, and no isolated piece. Disconnected grounded
pieces remain invalid even though each is grounded.

## Rotational, geometry, projection, and export checks

The fixture suite checks all transformed receiver sets through valid assemblies
at the four allowed quarter turns. It also checks that rotating the wedge from
0 to 90 degrees changes the collision result against `3024@(2,0,0)`; this
guards reflection/axis mishandling. Projection polygons are deterministic,
non-empty, and are the same nonrectangular exterior polygons used by collision
testing. The harness therefore does not substitute a rectangular bounding box
for wedge geometry.

The offline representation is compatible with existing rectangular bricks and
plates for this vertical-stud domain. It is not evidence that the production
exporter can emit or round-trip experimental `41769a`; that work remains
unimplemented.

## Coverage, limitations, and production isolation

Focused M12B tests cover valid assemblies, all listed invalid classes,
rotation-dependent collisions, bounding-box-only contact, solid collision,
receiver transforms, projection consistency, components, isolation, and part
counts. There were no observed false-positive or false-negative classifications
inside this limited test domain. The conservative solid prism can still reject
an unmodelled hollow-underside fit; this is a known intentional false-positive
possibility outside the tested accepted fixtures.

Unverified: clutch force, centre-of-mass stability, lateral bracing, insertion
feasibility, exact mould-wall clearance, any `41769b` or `41770` interface, and
all wedge-to-nonrectangular interactions. V4--V7 behaviour, frozen metrics,
catalogue eligibility (`fitterEnabled` remains false), API, workers, frontend,
and exporter remain unchanged.

## M12C decision and next step

**GO.** The required valid/invalid fixture gate passes without widening the
domain. The smallest safe M12C experiment is to add this one `41769a` variant
only to M9A's corrected offline candidate generation under the same five
receiver, quarter-turn, analytic-collision, direct-support, and no-export
constraints. It must retain the current production isolation until solver and
export validation are independently complete.


The complete focused command above completed with 74 passing tests in 1.62
seconds. All tests use local deterministic data and make no network,
paid-provider, cloud, or private-artifact calls.
